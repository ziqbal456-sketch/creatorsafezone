# Premiere Safe Margins for Vertical Video: Add a Social Overlay Check

Premiere's Safe Margins are useful composition guides, but a generic inset does not describe every social app's buttons, account information or expanded description. For vertical video, treat editor margins as one review aid and check the intended viewing layout separately.

This workflow is for an existing vertical edit. It helps you keep a temporary overlay aligned, review changing captions and remove the guide before delivery. It does not claim that one rectangle guarantees visibility across devices or placements.

## Start with the correct sequence

Confirm that the sequence matches the intended deliverable before positioning text. A 1080 × 1920 example overlay belongs on a matching 9:16 canvas. If your project uses another size or aspect ratio, adapt the guide deliberately rather than stretching it until it looks close.

Check the footage's scale and crop independently. A correctly aligned overlay cannot repair a face already cropped by a fill operation. Resolve those framing choices first so later caption adjustments refer to the final composition.

## Understand what Safe Margins show

[Adobe documents Safe Margins in the Source and Program monitors](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/get-started/source-and-program-monitor-adjustments/view-safe-zones-in-the-monitors.html). Enable them from the monitor's available settings or panel menu when you need an editor reference. The controls may vary with your version and workspace.

Do not infer a specific TikTok, Reels or Shorts interface from a generic margin guide. If you use a platform template or preset, inspect what it represents, who supplied it and which viewing state it covers. A template name alone is not evidence that it matches the placement you are publishing to.

## Add a temporary overlay track

Use a transparent PNG guide whose canvas dimensions match your sequence. The [custom overlay tutorial](https://creatorsafezone.com/guides/canva-video-safe-zone-overlay) includes an editable example and explains its custom margins. That example is a planning aid, not an official platform specification.

Import the image and place it on a dedicated video track above the edit. Align the complete image with the sequence frame; avoid a crop or transform that shifts its edges. Extend its duration over the section you want to inspect, and label the track clearly so another editor understands why it exists.

Keep the guide separate from the footage and text. This makes it easier to hide while judging visual quality and to remove before export. If the guide seems to move, check whether you transformed a nest or parent composition rather than only the footage beneath it.

## Review more than the opening frame

Pause at the longest caption, a two-line speaker exchange, any animated headline and the closing call to action. Watch the motion between these points as well: a title can start inside a guide and travel outside it during its animation.

Review the full text panel, not only its baseline. Background shapes, shadows and labels can occupy space beyond the letters. If a scene is crowded, shorten or reposition the text before reducing its size until it becomes difficult to read.

## Export and verify the delivered file

Hide or remove the temporary overlay track before exporting. Open the resulting video and confirm that no guide lines remain. Also confirm the dimensions and that captions belong to the correct revision.

Then load the export into the [comparison checker](https://creatorsafezone.com/check-all-platforms) to compare its composition with the configured platform regions. Finish in the destination's own preview for the intended placement. Neither the editor's margins nor a browser simulation substitutes for that final check, especially when descriptions or interface controls change.
