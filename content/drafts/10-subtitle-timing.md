# Subtitles Out of Sync? Separate a Fixed Delay from Timing Drift

When captions appear before or after speech, moving them higher on the screen will not solve the problem. First establish whether every caption is displaced by the same amount, whether the error grows over time, or whether only one section is wrong.

Work with a copy of the subtitle file or an editable caption track. Keep the final video beside it and give both matching revision names. Timing against an older cut is a common way to fix one section while making another worse.

## Compare three points in the final video

Choose a clear spoken word near the beginning, middle and end. Note when it is heard and when its caption appears. Use normal-speed playback as well as the editor's timeline; a still frame cannot show whether viewers have enough time to read the line.

For example, if the first, middle and final captions all arrive about 0.8 seconds late, a shared offset may be appropriate. If the first line is correct but the last arrives several seconds late, investigate drift or a mismatched edit instead. These numbers illustrate a diagnosis, not a recommended reading duration.

## Correct a fixed offset carefully

Use your caption editor's timing adjustment to shift the affected cues together. Late captions need to move earlier; early captions need to move later. For a cue that starts at 12.0 seconds and is 0.8 seconds late, the illustrative corrected start is 11.2 seconds.

Shift the end time by the same amount when preserving the cue's duration. Check cues near the beginning so they do not move before the video starts. Recheck the three sample points after the adjustment, and watch the surrounding dialogue rather than relying on the arithmetic alone.

## Investigate drift before changing everything

Confirm that the subtitle file belongs to the final export. Look for a trimmed introduction, inserted scene, removed pause or speed change. An error that begins immediately after one edit may require retiming only the later section, rather than stretching the entire track.

Do not change the video's frame rate merely because an online example mentions it. First compare the actual edit and caption timeline. If timing errors vary irregularly, reviewing individual cues or regenerating captions from the final audio may be more reliable than applying one global adjustment.

## Use the destination's timing tools where available

[YouTube documents editing caption timestamps](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734705?hl=en) in its captions editor, including an Edit timings option when timestamps are hidden. It also describes downloading caption files for editing and uploading the revised result. Follow the current interface for your account and selected language track.

Other destinations may provide different controls or accept different file types. Keep the editable subtitle source even if you eventually burn the words into the picture. Once captions are embedded in the video, changing their timing normally means exporting the video again from the project.

## Finish with timing and placement checks

Watch the entire corrected result, paying attention to speaker changes, short interjections and the final sentence. Check that cues do not linger over unrelated speech and that important sound information has not disappeared during editing. Confirm the correct caption track is selected in the viewing app.

CreatorSafeZone does not import or retime SRT files. After correcting timing in your editor, use a captioned video export in the [comparison checker](https://creatorsafezone.com/check-all-platforms) to assess placement. If the words are synchronized but hidden by controls, use the separate [caption placement guide](https://creatorsafezone.com/guides/captions-covered-by-buttons). Timing and visibility both need to work for the final viewer.
