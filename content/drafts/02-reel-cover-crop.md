# Reel Cover Text Cut Off? Check the Crop Before Redesigning

A cover can look balanced at full size and lose its headline when shown as a smaller tile. Start by separating the cover image from the playing video. A crop preview answers which part of an image remains visible; a video safe-zone preview answers where interface elements may cover that image. You may need both checks.

Do not rebuild your cover around an unexplained pixel template. Establish which surface is failing and test a crop that matches what you actually see.

## Compare the original with the affected preview

Save your original cover and inspect it outside Instagram. Then compare it with the cover-selection preview and the affected surface in your account. Note whether the top, bottom or sides are missing, whether the image is repositioned, and whether the text is merely too small to read.

If the whole headline is visible but unreadable at tile size, cropping is not the main problem. Shorten the headline, simplify its background and enlarge the lettering. If part of a letter is missing, investigate the crop and placement first.

Account interfaces and viewing surfaces can differ. The scenarios below are geometry exercises, not claims that Instagram universally uses a particular crop today.

## Understand what a centered crop removes

Take a 1080 × 1920 image and keep its full width. A centered 4:5 crop has a height of 1350 pixels, so it removes 285 pixels from the top and 285 from the bottom. A centered 3:4 crop has a height of 1440 pixels, removing 240 pixels at each end. A centered square retains 1080 pixels of height and removes 420 pixels at each end.

These calculations assume a centered crop with no additional zoom. Repositioning changes which edges lose content. They explain why text close to the top of a tall image can disappear in a shorter preview without the original image being damaged.

In the [crop previewer](https://creatorsafezone.com/instagram-grid-crop-previewer), use the available labelled scenario as a stress test. If it differs from your current Instagram preview, treat the in-app result as the relevant evidence and adjust the design accordingly. The tool does not reproduce every account's cover controls.

## Build the cover around the message

Put the essential headline and the identifying part of the subject near the center. Let decorative shapes and background detail extend toward the edges. Avoid making a border or line of text depend on a precise edge surviving.

Try a short headline such as “Three Lighting Fixes” instead of a sentence describing the entire video. That is an editorial choice, not an Instagram character limit. At tile size, check whether someone can identify the subject and read the headline without opening the image.

Save a separate cover version rather than editing the only copy. Compare the original and revised images in the same preview so you can tell whether your changes helped.

## Recheck the video separately

A cover that survives a crop does not prove that subtitles in the video clear interface controls. Open the playing video in the [Reels checker](https://creatorsafezone.com/instagram-reels-safe-zone) and inspect the hook, subtitles and final call to action. Keep cover checks and playback checks as separate steps in your publishing checklist.

## If the preview still looks different

Verify that the intended cover file was selected and that you are comparing the same viewing surface. Keep a screenshot of the preview and record the date and device. Avoid repeatedly resizing from memory: the comparison should identify exactly what moved or disappeared.
