# Burned-In vs Closed Captions: Prevent Double Subtitles on Social Video

Two sets of subtitles can compete for the same space: one exported inside your video and another drawn by the viewing app. Before moving text higher, identify which layer you can actually edit. A placement change cannot remove words already embedded in the picture.

This guide helps you choose a caption delivery workflow and review the final viewing experience. It does not assume that every social platform accepts a separate subtitle file or exposes the same caption controls.

## Identify the caption layers

Open the exported video in a local player with subtitle tracks disabled. If the words remain visible, they are burned into the picture. If they disappear, they were supplied by a separate track or player feature. Also check whether your editor exported a subtitle file beside the video.

[W3C distinguishes open captions from closed captions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/): open captions remain visible, while closed captions can be switched on and off. Captions should convey relevant speech and sounds; decorative headlines alone are not a replacement. A burned-in transcript with missing words can still leave viewers without the information they need.

## Choose the workflow before the final export

Use burned-in captions when you need a specific visual treatment to travel with the video. You control their position in the exported picture, but correcting a typo requires another video export. Keep the editable project so this correction is practical.

Use a separate caption track where the destination supports it and viewer control matters. Review that destination's current upload options rather than assuming an SRT file can be attached everywhere. The viewing app may determine the appearance and position, so an editor screenshot cannot confirm the final layout.

For a reusable project, keep a clean video export and a captioned version with clear filenames. For example, use “launch-clean-v2” and “launch-captioned-v2” alongside the matching subtitle file. This is an organizational suggestion, not a required platform naming convention.

## Diagnose double captions without removing access

1. Play the actual export locally and note which text is permanent.
2. Inspect the destination's upload preview and available caption settings.
3. Check whether automatic captions or an uploaded track add another visible layer.
4. Review both captions-on and captions-off states where the player permits them.
5. Choose the appropriate export and caption setup, then repeat the check.

Do not automatically delete the accessible track just to tidy a screenshot. First consider whether a clean video with a reviewed caption track meets the publishing need. If you retain burned-in text and a separate track, assess the combined viewing state and the information available when either layer is absent.

## Check placement separately from delivery

Load the captioned export into the [multi-platform checker](https://creatorsafezone.com/check-all-platforms). Pause on the longest line, a speaker change and the final call to action. Mark the full caption block, including its background panel, rather than just the central words.

The checker helps compare your exported composition with its configured guide regions. It does not import subtitle tracks or reproduce every viewer's caption settings. Follow the [caption obstruction guide](https://creatorsafezone.com/guides/captions-covered-by-buttons) if app buttons cover the permanent text, then verify the real destination preview.

## Final handoff checklist

Record which video version is intended for each destination and whether a separate caption file is required. Check spelling, speaker names, meaningful sounds and timing. Watch with sound off to see whether the message still makes sense, then watch with sound to catch mismatched words. Keep the clean master and editable captions together so future revisions do not require rebuilding the entire project.
