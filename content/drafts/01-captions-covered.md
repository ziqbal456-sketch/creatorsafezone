# Captions Covered by Buttons? Fix Text Placement in Reels, TikTok and Shorts

If subtitles look correct in your editor but disappear behind buttons after uploading, first check whether the problem is interface overlap or cropping. Interface overlap hides part of the picture beneath controls. Cropping removes part of the picture. Moving your caption can solve the first problem; the second may require changing your framing too.

This guide covers text you add to your video. Platform-generated captions are a separate layer and may behave differently.

## Identify which layer is causing the problem

Open the exported file outside the social app and pause at the affected line. If the full caption is already missing, return to the editing project. Check the text box, animation and export frame. A social safe-zone template will not repair text that was cut off during export.

If the exported caption is intact, compare the same moment inside the destination app. Note what covers it: the action buttons, account information, post description or another caption layer. Record the platform and viewing surface instead of assuming every viewer sees the same layout.

YouTube says automatic captions can contain transcription errors and should be reviewed. Fixing placement alone does not fix incorrect wording. [YouTube automatic-caption guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554?hl=en).

## Test the widest and tallest caption blocks

Choose three moments: your longest line, your tallest wrapped caption and a line displayed during a busy shot. Short opening captions can pass while later lines reach into the right-side controls.

Open the [comparison checker](https://creatorsafezone.com/check-all-platforms) with your exported video. Pause at each moment and draw a manual region around the entire caption block, including its background. These regions are markers you position yourself; the checker does not automatically detect or track your subtitles.

Use the platform interface previews as planning aids. Review the current margins and adjust them when your own in-app preview shows different coverage. A passing result means your marked region clears the selected boundaries, not that every app layout has been tested.

## Make the smallest useful edit

Move a caption away from the obstruction before shrinking it. If the right-hand controls cover the end of a line, narrow the text block or rebreak the sentence. If the bottom metadata covers the second line, raise the whole block and check that it does not obscure a face or demonstration.

For example, suppose your marked caption ends at Y:1514 and your chosen planning boundary ends at Y:1500. That is 14 pixels of overlap in that scenario. Moving it upward by 38 pixels leaves 24 pixels of clearance. Those numbers illustrate the calculation; they are not a platform specification.

Apply the change in your editor. Moving a marker in the checker does not move the caption inside your video. Export a new file and repeat the check.

## Check readability while the video moves

A caption can fit inside the boundary and still be difficult to read. Play the whole sentence at normal speed on a phone-sized display. Check the background behind every word, not just the paused frame. Try a solid or partly opaque caption background when bright footage defeats the lettering.

Avoid crowding the same space with a hook, subtitles and a call to action. Show secondary messages at a different moment where possible. Keep the spoken message understandable with sound muted, and review names, numbers and punctuation manually.

## Before you publish

- Confirm that the final exported file contains the revised captions.
- Check the longest caption and every change in caption position.
- Preview each destination separately, including any additional caption layer.
- Keep an editable master so a platform-specific version is easy to make.

For a worked example, use the [comparison tutorial](https://creatorsafezone.com/check-all-platforms#comparison-tutorial). The [measurement methodology](https://creatorsafezone.com/methodology) explains why planning margins cannot guarantee identical results across devices.
