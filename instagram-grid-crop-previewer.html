The Complete Guide to Instagram Reel Cropping: 1:1 Profile Grid vs 4:5 Home Feed

A video editor exports a stunning 9:16 Reel with cinematic titles at the top and bottom. It looks flawless when watched in full screen, but when visitors click on the creator’s Instagram profile, the titles are cut in half, the creator's forehead is sliced off, and the account grid looks amateurish.

Instagram Aspect Ratio Crop Hierarchy

9:16 Reels Player 1080 × 1920 (100% Canvas)

The full canvas viewed when a user taps into the Reel. Native UI overlays the right side and bottom.

4:5 Home Feed 1080 × 1350 (70.3% Canvas)

Crops 285px off the top and 285px off the bottom. High headlines get sliced off.

1:1 Profile Grid 1080 × 1080 (56.25% Canvas)

Crops 420px off the top and 420px off the bottom. Only the dead center square is visible.

How Professional Creators Design Dual-Purpose Covers

Instead of uploading separate images or letting Instagram select an awkward mid-motion frame, creators use the "Nest Technique":

  1. Create a 1080 × 1920 canvas in Canva, Photoshop, or Premiere.
  2. Turn on guides at Y: 420px and Y: 1500px to frame the center 1080×1080 square.
  3. Place your main headline, high-contrast face photo, and brand logo strictly within that center square.
  4. Fill the top 420px and bottom 420px with continuous background gradient or contextual footage.
  5. When viewed on your profile, it crops into a clean, legible card; when tapped, it expands to full screen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I adjust the crop of my Reel after publishing on Instagram?

Yes. Tap the three dots on your Reel → Manage → Edit → tap "Cover" → tap "Profile Grid". You can drag the 1:1 box slightly up or down to frame your subject, though designing within the center 1080×1080 safe area guarantees consistency without adjustments.

Why did Instagram test taller grid previews?

Instagram occasionally tests 4:5 and 3:4 profile grid ratios, but 1:1 remains the global default standard across iOS and Android. Keeping your primary headline inside the center 1080×1080 square ensures compatibility across all variants.
